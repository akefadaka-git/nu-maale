## Purpose

Establish the initial assessment mandate for a culturally grounded youth skills, enterprise, employment and livelihood development initiative serving Maale Woreda, South Omo, Ethiopia.

## Direction

The initiative is a blended, community-centred livelihood ecosystem. Technology is intended to support, not replace, practical training, mentorship, cooperatives, employers, buyers, extension services and local institutions.

The assessment is to determine which economic activities can generate sustainable, accessible and measurable income for Maale youth, and what combination of skills, productive assets, infrastructure, institutions, finance and market access is needed to make them viable.

## A1 scope

A1 covers cultural, linguistic, socioeconomic, economic, institutional, technical, financial-feasibility, inclusion, safeguarding and programme assessment. It is intended to produce an evidence-based specification and a reasoned recommendation for any later pilot.

Research methods may include youth and household surveys, key-informant interviews, focus groups, community and cultural consultations, buyer and employer interviews, value-chain observations, institutional mapping and feasibility analysis. Sampling must be justified from an appropriate population frame; a proposed pilot cohort is not a substitute for a statistically defensible research sample.

## Candidate livelihood pathways for assessment

- Beekeeping and honey value-chain development.
- Livestock-related services, including feed and animal-health support services.
- Technical and vocational services such as solar, electrical, construction, plumbing and equipment maintenance.
- Agriculture and irrigation.
- Other opportunities only where local demand, access, legality and feasibility are evidenced.

These are assessment candidates, not approved investments or selected pilot pathways. Historical prices and production figures must not be represented as current market evidence.
